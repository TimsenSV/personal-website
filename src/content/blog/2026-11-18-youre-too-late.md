# 01 - damn, this took a while

<!-- summary: A few years of lurking, and why I’m finally writing things down. -->

Hey, I’m Tim.

I’ll be straightforward. I’ve been a lurker for the last couple of years. Reading, watching, and saving pretty much anything about product development, startups, software, or tech. My bookmarks are going strong, thank you for asking. It wasn’t always like that, though.

## You might ask how I ended up here

I’m a product builder. Software products, to be precise. These days I build AI-enabled products for e-commerce. Before that I dabbled in construction tech, and before that I co-founded a machine learning startup (you know, when not everything was AI and there was nuance … ahhh, great times).

Anyways, I used to be more active in the startup scene, too. I joined startup events, won hackathons, started my own hackathons, mentored students, joined the workforce … and somewhere along the way I got quieter. Whenever I thought about sharing my thoughts publicly, I’d hear it back in my head as a startup cliché I’d already read ten times. That didn’t feel good enough. So I kept grinding in silence.

I figured I’d have something worth saying once I’d caught up. Every time I opened the feed, there was something else to catch up on.

## It’s time to break the cycle

Lurking is fine until the input piles up faster than you can do anything with it. I could spend a whole evening reading about how other people build things and still have no idea what I thought about any of it the next morning.

Meanwhile, at work, I kept running into the same questions. What will software look like in 1, 2, or 5 years? What are timeless design principles? Am I doing enough to keep up? A couple of days ago, when scrolling through my bookmarks (told you), I rediscovered this line from the GOAT, Jakob Nielsen.

> What was difficult for users twenty years ago continues to be difficult today.

— [Durability of Usability Guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/durability-of-usability-guidelines/), 2005

He was writing about usability, and I see the same thing in AI products (and work in general, for that matter). Frontier models are moving fast, but product people’s work is as relevant as ever. Because it’s about anticipating a future to build for, having taste, and rallying the troops. Things frontier models are arguably getting better at, but for now that job is still ours to do. If you’re unsure, just ask Claude (or DeepSeek, I don’t judge) about 10 innovative product ideas to work on.

Until then, there’s a massive gap between AI capabilities and adoption. What AI can do changes daily. Figuring out how to make it useful in someone’s actual day takes longer. I think that gap will be there for a while, whatever the AI influencers say. And oh boy, there’s a lot to build in it.

![A tongue-in-cheek sketch of the adoption gap. Model capabilities accelerate off the chart while user adoption grows steadily, from a sparkle button to a pilot to still exporting CSVs.](../images/blog/ai-adoption-gap.svg)

Until we have a mind-reading superchip in our brains that can materialize an idea (don’t think too dirty, please), product people’s key tasks remain unchanged:

- UX is still relevant
- UI is still relevant
- Adoption metrics, personal touch, and taste are still relevant
- Managing people (alongside bots) is still relevant

Form factors will change, product categories will die, and others will arise from seemingly nowhere. That’s fine. That’s the nature of things.

## One learning at a time

I’m someone who tends to think too fast for their own good. I regularly stumble over my own thoughts right into a puddle filled with impatience.

So I’m going to write things down. Slow down. Think about the things that matter, and share some of those thoughts with you, assuming you ever find these words.

Low stress. No FOMO. One learning at a time. You might disagree with some of it, and some of it might just be wrong. That’s okay. I’d like to leave myself enough room to change my mind.

Anyway, I’m already late. Might as well start.

See ya.
